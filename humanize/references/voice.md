# Voice profiles

"Sounds human" is a floor. "Sounds like me" is the actual goal for anything published under a name.

## Building a profile

Ask for three to five samples the user wrote themselves and liked — not their best work, their typical work. Published pieces, Slack messages, and emails all work; a mix is better than three of the same kind.

Read them for:

**Sentence length** — average, and how much it swings. Some people write 12-word sentences throughout. Others run 40 words then 4.

**Openings** — how they start paragraphs and pieces. Straight into the claim? A scene? A question?

**Contractions and register** — count them. Some careful writers use none and it isn't stiffness, it's them.

**Punctuation habits** — em dashes, semicolons, parentheses, ellipses, exclamation points. These are among the most individual markers a writer has.

**Vocabulary range** — do they reach for unusual words or stay plain? Any words they use constantly?

**Hedging** — confident and declarative, or qualified and careful?

**Humor** — dry, absent, self-deprecating, absurd? Where does it land in a piece?

**Structural habits** — lists vs. prose, headers vs. flow, how they close.

**What they never do** — often more diagnostic than what they do. No exclamation points. Never opens with a question. Never uses bullet lists.

## Storing it

Write the profile to `voice-profile.md` in the working folder, as observations with examples pulled from the samples:

```
Sentence length: mostly 15-25 words, with occasional 5-word sentences for emphasis.
  "That was the whole plan."
Punctuation: heavy em-dash user — roughly one per paragraph. No semicolons anywhere.
Register: contractions throughout, even in client work.
Never: rhetorical questions, exclamation points, three-item lists.
```

Load it during Pass 0 on subsequent jobs. Re-check it every few months; voices drift.

## Applying it

The profile is a target, not a costume. Apply it to sentence construction, rhythm, and word choice. Do not use it to generate content the user didn't write — matching someone's voice makes fabricated details *more* dangerous, because the fake anecdote now sounds convincingly like theirs.

If the source text conflicts with the profile — the user's draft is formal, their profile is casual — follow the draft. They may be writing for a different audience on purpose. Note the mismatch in your output instead of silently overriding it.

## No profile available

Default to a neutral human register: contractions on, varied rhythm, direct claims, no performance. Say in your output note that you used a default voice and offer to build a profile from samples.
